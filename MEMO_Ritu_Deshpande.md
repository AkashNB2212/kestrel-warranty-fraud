**To:** Ritu Deshpande, Head of D2C Operations · **Cc:** Farhan Sheikh, Meenal Joshi · **From:** Kabir's team · **Re:** Warranty fraud flag

### Decision
**Use the fraud flag to choose the 40 claims the investigation desk checks each month, starting next week. Do not judge it by "accuracy above 97%".**

### Why not 97% accuracy
Only about 1 claim in 80 is fraud. A system that flags nothing at all is already "98.7% accurate" on our 15 months of claims, while catching zero fraud. On June, the latest month we can check, our flag reaches **97.5%** at its strictest setting but catches only 6 of 22 frauds. Used the way the desk actually works (check the 40 riskiest claims a month), it caught **14 of the 22 June frauds**, and **1 in 3 claims it sent to the desk was fraud**. That compares with 1 in 32 at random. The best simple rule we tried, "check the newest outlets first", caught 4. I suggest the board sees two numbers: **share of fraud caught** and **rupees stopped per claim checked**.

### What changed
Fraud is up in **number**: 8 a month before May, 22 in June. It is down in **rupees**: about ₹44,000 a month before, ₹33,600 a month since May. Since claims under ₹2,000 started being paid without inspection on 1 May, 34 of 36 frauds are small claims that skip inspection. Claim amounts have also started bunching just under the limit: no claim was ever exactly ₹1,995 before May, and 87 have been since (8 of them fraud). Before May, fraud was large, inspected claims at about seven long-standing franchises, and those outlets have had no fraud since.

### The rupees (June, checked against real outcomes)
| | per month |
|---|---|
| Fraud paid out with no checks | ₹45,673 |
| Fraud stopped by checking the flag's top 40 | **₹20,143** (₹504 per claim checked) |
| Goodwill for 26 genuine customers held | −₹9,880 |
| **Net saving** | **₹10,263** (about ₹1.2 lakh a year) |

If each check also costs a ₹260 service contact, the claim-by-claim saving is about break-even. **The bigger win is the outlet pattern the flag exposes.**

### Your hunch about new partners
**Partly right, and only since May.** Since 1 May, claims from outlets under a year old are 16% fraud, against 0.2% for older outlets, and that holds after allowing for claim size and route. But it is **five outlets out of 45 new ones**: SP3160 Pune, SP3232 Jaipur, SP3318 Hubballi, SP3129 Chennai and SP3319 Warangal. They produced 29 of the 34 frauds (₹42,010), and more than half of their claims were fraud. The other 40 new outlets are mostly fine, as Meenal said. Before May, new outlets were actually the cleanest.

### What to do next week
1. **Turn off auto-approval for those five outlets.** Their 63 claims from July–September (₹1.07 lakh claimed) should be checked before any further payout.
2. **Start the queue.** The desk takes the 40 highest-scored unpaid claims each month. The screen shows the reasons for each claim.
3. **Ask Finance for the cost of one inspection**, then decide whether outlets under a year old should send a photo and serial before auto-approval.
4. **Close the 215 open investigations and record the date each case is closed.** The flag learns from these outcomes, and it misses brand-new outlets until they have some.
5. **Retrain monthly, and immediately after any policy change.** A version trained before 1 May missed every May fraud.
